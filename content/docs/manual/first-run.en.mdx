The first time you open PKU Harness, the app shows a few intro pages, asks you to accept the terms,
and then takes you through six setup steps. This page describes each step in order.

Your progress is saved after every step. If you leave the app halfway, it opens on the same step
next time. To go back one step, tap the back arrow at the top left or press Android's back button.
The progress bar at the top shows which step you are on.

<Callout title="Set up the AI first">
  Everything you do in the Chat tab needs an AI model service, and the app doesn't include one. If
  you don't have an API key yet, read [AI provider](/en/docs/manual/ai-model) first. It explains,
  step by step, how to get a key from DeepSeek, Alibaba Cloud Bailian or Baidu Qianfan. Getting a
  key takes about ten minutes; you can do it on your phone's browser.
</Callout>

## Intro and terms

There are five intro pages. Swipe or tap **Continue** to move on, or tap **Skip intro** to jump to
the last page.

The last page has a checkbox: "I agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy…". Tap **Terms of
Service** or **Privacy Policy** below to read them in full. **Agree and continue** becomes
available once the box is checked.

After you agree, the AI can act on campus services for you within the permissions you grant. You
can change each plugin's permissions later; see
[Plugins and permissions](/en/docs/manual/plugins#permissions).

## Step 1: Connect an AI provider

The assistant needs a large language model to work. You need an API key from a model provider for
this step. A key is a long password-like string, often starting with `sk-`.

1. If there is more than one AI provider plugin, pick one at the top: **DeepSeek** or
   **OpenAI-compatible**. DeepSeek is the default. Choose **OpenAI-compatible** for Alibaba Cloud
   Bailian, Baidu Qianfan and other services.
2. For **OpenAI-compatible**, fill in **Base URL**. The address for each provider is in
   [AI provider](/en/docs/manual/ai-model#recommended-providers).
3. Paste your key into **API key**. The buttons on the right paste from the clipboard and show or
   hide the key.
4. Tap **Verify and connect**. The app sends one request to the provider with this key. On success
   it shows "Connected · N models available". On failure it says why, for example that the key is
   invalid or the account balance is too low.
5. Under **Model**, pick a model. If the provider returned no model list, type the model's name in
   **Model ID**.
6. Tap **Continue**.

The key stays on this phone and is only sent to the provider you chose.

## Step 2: Sign in to PKU

1. Enter your IAAA account, the one you use for the portal, in **Student or staff ID** and
   **Password**.
2. Tap **Sign in**.
3. If the university asks for an SMS code or an authenticator code, the app shows a prompt. Enter
   the code and tap **Verify**.
4. When you are signed in, the page greets you by name. Tap **Continue**.

If you see a network error, a service that needs the campus network may be out of reach. See
[Troubleshooting](/en/docs/manual/troubleshooting).

## Step 3: Authenticator

This step makes the app your IAAA mobile authenticator (手机令牌). After that, when a university
sign-in asks for an authenticator code, the app fills it in, and an **Authenticator** widget appears
on your dashboard. The widget hides the code until you tap it; see
[Campus account and authenticator](/en/docs/manual/campus-account#authenticator).

This step needs your PKU sign-in. If you didn't sign in during step 2, it only shows an explanation
and a **Continue** button.

1. Read the notes on the page. Setting this up makes any authenticator you set up before in another
   app (such as FreeOTP or Google Authenticator) stop working.
2. Check **I understand my old authenticator will stop working**.
3. Tap **Send SMS code**. The code goes to the phone number on your account.
4. Enter it in **SMS code** and tap **Bind**.

If you don't want to set it up now, tap **Not now**. You can do it later under
**Settings → Plugins → Authenticator**.

## Step 4: Choose plugins

The page lists the built-in plugins in three groups: **Campus services**, **Productivity** and
**System**. Use the switch on each row to turn a plugin on or off. Changes apply right away.

- Some plugins carry labels. **Needs sign-in** means you need to sign in to your PKU account first,
  **On campus only** means it only works on the campus network, and **Notifications** and
  **Background** mean it sends notifications or runs in the background.
- Plugins in **System** are part of the app and always on.

Tap **Continue** when you are done. You can change this any time in **Settings → Plugins**.

## Step 5: Get reminders on time

Reminders and scheduled checks run on your phone and are woken by the system alarm. This step has
three items.

| Item | Label | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Notifications** | Required | Tap **Allow** and allow notifications in the system dialog. If they were blocked, the button reads **Open settings** |
| **Alarms & reminders** | Required | Tap **Turn on** and allow PKU Harness in Android's "Alarms & reminders" setting |
| **Unrestricted battery** | Recommended | Tap **Allow**, then **Allow** in the system dialog that opens |

Without notifications, reminders and alerts don't show up. Without alarms & reminders, Android may
deliver reminders minutes late or more. Unrestricted battery lets scheduled checks, such as a
morning balance check, run on time in the background.

You can change these permissions later in **Settings → Reminders and alerts**.

## Step 6: Let the assistant use your other apps [#step-6-copilot]

This step introduces **Copilot**, which lets the assistant open and operate other apps on your
phone, such as the browser or a food delivery app. It only appears on Android.

The page lists what Copilot needs and marks each item **Done** or **To do**:

- **Phone helper (Wireless debugging pairing)**
- **Accessibility permission**
- **Apps it may use**

Tap **Set up Copilot** to open the Copilot screen and go through the setup there. When you come back
and everything is done, the page shows **Copilot is ready**; tap **Continue**. If the phone helper
is connected but accessibility is off, the **Turn on accessibility** button turns it on here.

Setup takes a few minutes and uses Android's developer options. If that sounds unfamiliar, tap
**Set up later** and follow [Copilot](/en/docs/manual/copilot) when you have time. You can also start
it later by typing `/copilot` in chat.

## Finish

The last page summarizes the setup: whether an **AI** provider is connected, whether your
**Account** is signed in, how many **Services** are turned on, whether **Reminders** arrive on time,
and whether **Copilot** is ready.

If an AI provider is connected, **Try asking** lists a few questions, such as "How much is left on
my campus card?". Tapping one puts it in the chat's message box; you decide whether to send it. Tap
**Get started** to open the Chat tab.

## What "Set up later" does

Steps 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 have a **Set up later** button at the bottom; step 3 has **Not now**. Tapping
it moves to the next step without saving anything you entered on this one. You can finish skipped
items later.

| Step | Where to set it up later |
| --- | --- |
| Connect an AI provider | Settings → AI provider |
| Sign in to PKU | Settings → Campus account |
| Authenticator | Settings → Plugins → Authenticator |
| Choose plugins | Settings → Plugins |
| Reminder permissions | Settings → Reminders and alerts |
| Copilot | `/copilot` in chat, or Settings → Plugins → Copilot |

You can enter the app without an AI provider, but chat won't work; the Chat tab shows a
**Connect AI service** button.

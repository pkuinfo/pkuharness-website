The **Copilot** plugin lets the assistant operate other apps on your phone. It opens and operates
them on a separate virtual screen, so you can keep using your phone at the same time. It can only
operate the apps you allow. This page explains how to set it up and use it.

Copilot is available on Android only. It needs two things to be ready: the **Phone helper**
(connected through Android's wireless debugging) and the Copilot accessibility service.

## Open the Copilot screen

Type `/copilot` in chat, or open **Settings → Plugins → Copilot** and, under **Open**, tap
**Copilot**.

## Connect the phone helper

The **Phone helper** is a helper program that the app starts on this phone through Android's
wireless debugging. It connects only to this phone itself, never to a computer or server. The
phone must be on Wi-Fi.

**Before you start**, at the top of the Copilot screen, lists the steps in order and shows which
are done.

1. Tap **Turn on Wireless debugging**. The first time, Android asks whether PKU Harness may send
   notifications: choose **Allow**, because you enter the pairing code in a notification. The app
   then opens **Wireless debugging** in Developer options. If Developer options are not on yet, the
   button reads **Open About phone**: tap **Build number** seven times, then go back and open
   **Developer options** (usually under **System**).
2. Turn on **Wireless debugging**. If asked, allow the current network.
3. Tap **Wireless debugging**, then **Pair device with pairing code**, and keep that dialog open.
4. Pull down the notification shade. In the Copilot notification, tap **Enter code**, type the six
   digits shown in the dialog and send. You don't need to enter a port or use split screen.

When the notification says the phone helper is connected, pairing is done.

If the notification says the code was wrong or the dialog closed, tap **Pair device with pairing
code** again and enter the new code in the same notification.

If you don't allow notifications, or the notification doesn't appear, tap **Enter code manually**
and enter the code on the Copilot screen. You can leave **IP address & Port** empty and the app
finds it, or paste the address shown in the pairing dialog, for example `192.168.1.5:37145`.

A phone that has paired once doesn't need to pair again after a disconnect. The next time you
open PKU Harness, the phone helper turns Wireless debugging back on and reconnects by itself.

## Enable accessibility

Once the phone helper is connected, the app turns on the Copilot accessibility service by itself;
you don't need to open system settings. It also allows Android 13's restricted settings for itself
and adds itself to the battery optimization allowlist so it can keep running in the background.

If Android force-stops PKU Harness, it also turns off its accessibility service. While Copilot is
on and the phone helper is connected, the app turns it back on when you start the next task or
open the Copilot screen.

If you turn the accessibility service off yourself in system settings, the **Turn on
accessibility** step shows a **Turn on** button.

## Choose allowed apps

Copilot only operates the apps you allow.

1. Under **App access**, choose **All apps**. Use **Search apps** to find one.
2. Turn on the switch next to each app you want to allow. You can choose up to 20 apps.
3. Your choices save automatically. The **Allowed apps** tab lists the apps you chose.

Entries marked "System dialogs · opened by another app" are system screens, such as a settings
screen another app opens. Allow them only if Copilot needs to work in those screens.

If an app you allowed has been uninstalled, the screen lists it under **Unavailable apps**. Tap
**Remove unavailable apps** before enabling Copilot again.

If a task needs an app you haven't allowed, the assistant asks you first: “Copilot” wants to use,
with the app's name. Choose **Allow once** (this task only, not saved), **Always allow** (saved to
the allowed list) or **Don't allow** (the assistant stops, tells you, and doesn't ask again). Only
your tap can grant access; the assistant can't widen it by itself. When you're not in PKU Harness,
the request comes as a notification or in the floating window.

## Screenshots (optional)

Some apps, such as WeChat, hide their contents from Copilot. For those, you can turn on **Let the AI
see screenshots** on the Copilot screen (off by default):

- The assistant takes a screenshot of the app Copilot is operating, adds a grid with coordinate
  labels, sends it to your AI provider, and then taps or types at a point. Every such tap or entry
  asks for your confirmation.
- Only Copilot's separate screen is captured, never the screen you are using, and never protected
  screens such as passwords or payments. A screenshot is used for one reply only and is not saved
  in the chat.
- The selected model must read images, for example DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.
- WeChat's terms forbid automated operation, and Tencent may restrict accounts that use automation
  tools. Judge the risk yourself.

## Enable Copilot

Once the phone helper is connected, accessibility is on and at least one app is allowed, tap **Turn
on Copilot** in the last step. The screen shows **Copilot is ready**; tap **Go to chat** to start.
Afterwards you can turn Copilot off or on again with the **Enable Copilot** switch at the top.

After that, describe a task in chat, for example "Search for the PKU library in the browser". You
don't need to start each task by hand. The assistant opens the allowed apps on the separate screen
and operates them, and a floating window shows its progress.

## Confirm, pause and take over

Copilot's read actions run directly. Tapping, typing, submitting and other actions ask for your
confirmation each time by default.

- While the app is in the foreground, the confirmation appears in the app.
- While the app is in the background, it appears in the floating window. Tap **Allow once** or
  **Deny**.

The floating window can also **Pause** and **Resume**, preview the current screen, **Stop**, and
take over.

- Tap **Minimize** at the top right to shrink the floating window to a small round icon. The dot on
  the icon shows what Copilot is doing: red while it works, orange when it needs your confirmation
  or attention, grey while it is paused or you have control. Drag the icon to move it; it settles
  against the nearer screen edge. Tap it to open the floating window again.
- When Copilot needs your confirmation, the floating window opens by itself, so a minimized icon
  never leaves a request unseen.
- Drag the handle at the bottom right of the preview to make it larger or smaller. The app keeps the
  size you choose.

For passwords, protected screens and payment confirmations, the assistant hands the app over to you
on your main screen. You can also tap **Take over** on the Copilot screen at any time. When you are
done, tap **Return control to Copilot** on the Copilot screen.

The assistant works by reading the text and controls on the app's screen, and that content is sent
to your AI provider as part of the chat. The preview images in the floating window and on the
Copilot screen are only shown on your phone and are never sent to the AI model.

## Stop

Any of these ends the current phone task right away:

- Tap **Stop** in the floating window.
- Tap **Stop Copilot** under **Current task** on the Copilot screen.
- Turn off **Enable Copilot**. Your list of allowed apps is kept.

After you stop it, the current reply can't start the task again by itself. Send a new message to
start a new task.

Scheduled tasks can't use Copilot.

The **Copilot** plugin lets the assistant operate other apps on your phone. It opens and operates
them on a separate virtual screen, so you can keep using your phone at the same time. It can only
operate the apps you allow. This page explains how to set it up and use it.

Copilot is available on Android only. It needs two things to be ready: the **Phone helper**
(connected through Android's wireless debugging) and the Copilot accessibility service.

## Open the Copilot screen

Type `/copilot` in chat, or open **Settings → Plugins → Copilot** and, under **Open**, tap
**Copilot**.

## Connect the phone helper

The phone helper is a small program the app starts through Android's wireless debugging. Your phone
needs to be on Wi-Fi.

1. On the Copilot screen, expand **Device connection** and choose **Pair device**.
2. Show PKU Harness and Android's Settings in split screen.
3. Tap **Open developer options**. In Developer options, turn on **Wireless debugging**, then open
   **Pair device with pairing code**. If your phone doesn't have Developer options yet, turn them on
   in Android's settings first.
4. Keep the pairing dialog open, and enter the port and six-digit code it shows in
   **Pairing port** and **Pairing code**.
5. Tap **Pair phone**.

When it is connected, the **Phone helper** row shows **Connected**.

If the phone helper disconnects, for example because wireless debugging was turned off, a phone that
was paired before doesn't need to pair again. Check that wireless debugging is on, choose
**Reconnect** and tap **Reconnect**. If the app can't find the port, enter the port shown on the
main Wireless debugging screen in **Connection port**.

If the screen says the code is wrong, expired or its dialog closed, open the pairing dialog again
and retry with the new code.

## Enable accessibility

With the phone helper connected, tap **Enable Copilot accessibility**. The app turns the service on
directly, without a trip to Android's settings.

Without the phone helper, find **Copilot** in Android's accessibility settings and turn it on
yourself. For apps installed from an APK, Android may say this is a restricted setting and refuse.
In that case:

1. On the Copilot screen, tap **Open App info**.
2. Tap ⋮ in the top corner and choose **Allow restricted settings**.
3. Go back to Android's accessibility settings and turn on **Copilot**.

When both are ready, **Device connection** says "Phone helper and accessibility are ready".

## Choose allowed apps

Copilot only operates the apps you allow.

1. Under **App access**, choose **All apps**. Use **Search apps** to find one.
2. Turn on the switch next to each app you want to allow. You can choose up to 20 apps.
3. Your choices save automatically. The **Allowed apps** tab lists the apps you chose.

Entries marked "System dialogs · opened by another app" are system screens, such as a settings
screen another app opens. Allow them only if Copilot needs to work in those screens.

If an app you allowed has been uninstalled, the screen lists it under **Unavailable apps**. Tap
**Remove unavailable apps** before enabling Copilot again.

## Enable Copilot

Turn on the **Enable Copilot** switch at the top of the screen. It can be turned on once the device
connection is ready and at least one app is allowed.

After that, describe a task in chat, for example "Search for the PKU library in the browser". You
don't need to start each task by hand. The assistant opens the allowed apps on the separate screen
and operates them, and a floating window shows its progress.

## Confirm, pause and take over

Copilot's read actions run directly. Tapping, typing, submitting and other actions ask for your
confirmation each time by default.

- While the app is in the foreground, the confirmation appears in the app.
- While the app is in the background, it appears in the floating window. Tap **Allow once** or
  **Deny**.

The floating window can also **Pause** and **Resume**, preview the current screen, **Stop**, and
take over.

For passwords, protected screens and payment confirmations, the assistant hands the app over to you
on your main screen. You can also tap **Take over** on the Copilot screen at any time. When you are
done, tap **Return control to Copilot** on the Copilot screen.

The assistant works by reading the text and controls on the app's screen, and that content is sent
to your AI provider as part of the chat. The preview images in the floating window and on the
Copilot screen are only shown on your phone and are never sent to the AI model.

## Stop

Any of these ends the current phone task right away:

- Tap **Stop** in the floating window.
- Tap **Stop Copilot** under **Current task** on the Copilot screen.
- Turn off **Enable Copilot**. Your list of allowed apps is kept.

After you stop it, the current reply can't start the task again by itself. Send a new message to
start a new task.

Scheduled tasks can't use Copilot.
